The Concept

Quantifying what cannot be measured

Quantifying Kindness is a live installation in which the artist sits motionless before a gallery audience. A camera reads every face that approaches: the micro-flickers of muscle, the brief surrender of a guarded expression, the warmth that escapes before the mind can suppress it.

A real-time computer vision pipeline converts these expressions into a numerical "Kindness Index." Each score above a threshold triggers a micro-donation — £1 to a charitable cause of the institution's choosing. The gallery itself becomes a site of measurable generosity.

"Can computational quantification catalyze real-world altruism? And if kindness has a number — does naming it make it more or less real?"
Artist Statement, 2024

The work asks whether the act of measurement transforms the thing measured — whether surveillance, usually an instrument of control, can become an engine of compassion.

How It Works

Four moments in a feedback loop

01

Artist is Present

The artist sits in absolute stillness before an empty chair. Visitors approach and sit — or simply pass. The camera watches both.

02

Machine Reads

A ResNet-based model (face-api.js / TinyFaceDetector) detects faces and classifies seven micro-expression categories at 30 fps.

03

Index Computed

Expression probabilities are weighted and smoothed over a rolling 3-second window to yield the Kindness Index: a float from 1.0 to 10.0.

04

Giving Triggered

When the index crosses 7.0, a micro-donation is logged in real time. The audience can watch the aggregate rise on a public display.

Inspiration

After Marina Abramović's
The Artist is Present

From 2010 to 2010, Abramović sat in silence for 736.5 hours at MoMA, inviting strangers to occupy the chair opposite her. The work demonstrated that sustained, undivided attention — in an age of distraction — is itself a radical act.

Quantifying Kindness inherits the structure of that encounter: the stillness, the face-to-face confrontation, the gallery as a container for vulnerability. But it adds a computational layer: what if the moment of connection were also a moment of measurement?

The question is not whether the algorithm is accurate. The question is what it means to try — to build a machine that searches a stranger's face for evidence of warmth.

"The hardest thing is to do something which is close to nothing."
Marina Abramović
Generative Artifact

Anonymized emotional trajectories

After the exhibition closes, the timestamped, anonymized kindness scores of every visitor are transformed into a unique visual artifact. No photographs. No names. Only the shape of feeling over time.

Each arc represents a single visitor encounter. Its color encodes their peak score; its curvature, the rate of change. Thousands of arcs accumulate into something that resembles a weather system — turbulent, patterned, alive.

The artifact is printed at 150 × 150 cm and presented alongside the exhibition as a permanent record of the collective emotional landscape of that specific space, at that specific moment in history.
